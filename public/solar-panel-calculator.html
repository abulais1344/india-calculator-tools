How Much Solar Do You Need?

In most of India, 1 kW of rooftop solar produces about 4 units (kWh) a day, or roughly 120 units a month. Sunny regions such as Rajasthan and Gujarat often get more, while hilly areas and North India in winter get less. To size your system, divide your monthly units by 120.

Example: a home using 300 units a month at ₹7 per unit needs about a 2.5 kW system, which is about 5 panels of 550 W and 250 sq ft of shadow-free roof. At ₹60,000 per kW it costs about ₹1.5 lakh. The central subsidy is ₹69,000, so you pay about ₹81,000. It saves about ₹2,100 a month and pays for itself in roughly 3.2 years.

How the Calculator Works

  • System size = monthly units ÷ (generation per kW per day × 30), rounded up to the nearest 0.5 kW.
  • Panels assume 550 W modules, the common size for home systems. Roof area assumes about 100 sq ft per kW.
  • Subsidy follows the PM Surya Ghar central rates above.
  • Saving = solar units used (up to your consumption) × your rate per unit. Fixed charges on your bill stay the same, and export credit under net metering depends on your state, so real savings can differ slightly.
  • Payback = cost after subsidy ÷ yearly saving. Electricity tariffs usually rise over time, which shortens the real payback.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many kW of solar do I need for my home?

Divide your monthly electricity units by about 120, because 1 kW of rooftop solar produces roughly 4 units a day or 120 units a month in most of India. For example, a home using 300 units a month needs about a 2.5 kW system.

How much subsidy is available for rooftop solar in India?

Under PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, residential consumers get ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, up to a maximum of ₹78,000 for systems of 3 kW or more. Special category states such as the North-Eastern states, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand and J&K get ₹33,000 and ₹19,800 per kW. Some states add their own top-up.

How much does a rooftop solar system cost in India?

Installed prices in 2026 are typically around ₹55,000 to ₹70,000 per kW before subsidy, depending on the installer, panel type and location. A 3 kW system costing about ₹1.8 lakh would cost about ₹1.02 lakh after the ₹78,000 central subsidy.

How long does rooftop solar take to pay back?

With the central subsidy, many homes recover the cost in roughly 3 to 6 years, depending on electricity tariff, sunlight and system price. Solar panels usually last 25 years, so the remaining years are mostly savings.
