How the Calculator Works

  • Starts from about 1 ton per 130 sq ft for a normal room with a 10 ft ceiling, a common rule of thumb for Indian summers.
  • Scales up for higher ceilings, afternoon sun and top-floor rooms (up to +40%).
  • Adds about 0.05 ton (600 BTU per hour) for each person beyond two, and half a ton for a kitchen.
  • Rounds up to the nearest size sold: 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 or 3 ton.

Example

A 12 × 12 ft bedroom (144 sq ft) with a 10 ft ceiling and 2 people needs about 1.1 ton, so a 1.5 ton AC. The same room on the top floor needs about 1.4 ton, which is still 1.5 ton.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which AC is best for a 150 sq ft room?

As a rule of thumb, a 1.5 ton AC for a 150 sq ft room with a normal 10 ft ceiling. Rooms up to about 130 sq ft usually need 1 ton, and about 195–260 sq ft need 2 tons. Top-floor rooms, west-facing walls and more people push the size up.

What does 1 ton mean in an AC?

One ton of cooling removes 12,000 BTU of heat per hour (about 3.5 kW). It has nothing to do with the AC's weight.

Is a bigger AC better?

Not always. An oversized AC cools quickly but switches on and off often and removes less humidity; an undersized AC runs constantly and struggles on hot days. Choose the size that matches your room, and prefer an inverter AC with a good star rating.
