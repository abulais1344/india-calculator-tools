How to Calculate Units

Units (kWh) = watts × hours ÷ 1,000. One unit is 1,000 watts running for one hour.

Example household: two 75 W fans for 12 hours (1.8 units), a 1.5 ton inverter AC for 6 hours (6.6 units), a fridge (about 1 unit), six LED bulbs for 6 hours (0.3 units), a TV for 4 hours (0.3 units) and a geyser for 30 minutes (1 unit) use about 11 units a day, or 330 units a month. At ₹7 per unit, that is about ₹2,310 a month in energy charges, and the AC alone is 60% of it.

Ways to Cut Your Bill

  • Set the AC to 24–26°C. Each degree lower makes the compressor work noticeably harder.
  • Choose inverter ACs and BLDC fans when replacing old ones. They use far less power for the same comfort.
  • Check the BEE star label before buying. It shows yearly units, so you can compare models directly.
  • Heat water only when needed. A geyser left on is one of the biggest hidden loads in winter.
  • Consider rooftop solar. 1 kW of solar makes about 120 units a month, and the PM Surya Ghar subsidy covers up to ₹78,000.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many units does a 1.5 ton AC consume per hour?

Typically about 1.1 to 1.5 units per hour. A 1.5 ton inverter AC averages around 1.1 units an hour once the room is cool, while an older non-inverter model draws about 1.5 units. Running 6 hours a day, that is roughly 200 to 270 units a month.

How many units does a ceiling fan use?

A regular 75 W ceiling fan uses 0.075 units an hour, about 27 units a month at 12 hours a day. An energy-efficient BLDC fan of about 30 W uses less than half of that.

How many units does a fridge consume per day?

A typical double-door frost-free fridge of about 250 litres uses around 1 unit a day, or about 30 units a month. The exact figure depends on its star rating, size and room temperature; the yearly units are printed on the BEE star label.

How do I calculate electricity units for an appliance?

Units (kWh) = watts × hours of use ÷ 1,000. For example, a 2,000 W geyser used for 30 minutes uses 2,000 × 0.5 ÷ 1,000 = 1 unit.
