What "Age As On" Means

Government job and exam notifications check your age on one fixed cut-off date, written as "age as on 01-08-2026" or "age as on 01-01-2027". Your age on the day you fill the form or sit the exam does not matter. Only your age on the cut-off date is compared with the minimum and maximum limits.

Enter your date of birth and the cut-off date from the notification. The calculator shows your exact age in years, months and days on that date. Add the minimum and maximum age and your category to see whether you fall within the limit after relaxation.

Example

Suppose your date of birth is 15 March 2001 and the notification says age limit 18–27 years as on 01-08-2026. On 1 August 2026 you will be 25 years, 4 months and 17 days old, which is within the 18–27 limit. An OBC (NCL) candidate gets 3 extra years, so their upper limit becomes 30.

Check the "Born Between" Dates

Most notifications also state the limits as an exact birth date range, for example "candidates should have been born not before 02-08-1999 and not later than 01-08-2008". That range is final. If your age is right at the minimum or maximum limit, compare your date of birth directly with those dates instead of relying on years alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "age as on date" mean in a job notification?

It is the fixed date on which your age is checked against the minimum and maximum age limits, for example "age as on 01-08-2026". Your age on the day you apply or take the exam does not matter; only your age on this cut-off date counts.

How much age relaxation do OBC, SC/ST and PwBD candidates get?

For most central government recruitment, the upper age limit is relaxed by 3 years for OBC (non-creamy layer), 5 years for SC/ST, and 10 years for PwBD (General), with more for PwBD candidates who are also OBC or SC/ST. Ex-servicemen and state government rules differ, so always confirm in the official notification.

Why should I check the "born not before" and "not later than" dates?

Notifications usually define age limits through exact birth date ranges, such as "born not before 02-08-1999 and not later than 01-08-2008". Those dates are final. If your age is right at a limit, compare your date of birth directly with that range.

Can I check my age on a future cut-off date?

Yes. Enter any past or future cut-off date. This lets you check eligibility for exams that will be announced later, for example the next year's recruitment cycle.
