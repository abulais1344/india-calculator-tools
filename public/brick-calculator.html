How the Brick Calculator Works

  1. Wall volume = length × height × thickness, minus doors and windows.
  2. Bricks = wall volume ÷ volume of one brick with a 10 mm mortar joint on each side, plus wastage.
  3. Wet mortar = wall volume − volume of the bricks themselves. Dry mortar = wet mortar × 1.33 (allowing for voids and bulking).
  4. Cement = dry mortar × 1 ÷ (1 + sand ratio), at 1,440 kg per m³ and 50 kg per bag. Sand = dry mortar × sand ratio ÷ (1 + sand ratio).

Example

A 10 ft × 10 ft wall, 9 inches thick, with standard bricks and 1:6 mortar has 2.12 m³ of brickwork. It needs about 868 bricks (about 911 with 5% wastage), 2.6 bags of cement and about 19 cft of sand.

Quick Reference

  • About 410 standard bricks or 500 modular bricks per m³ of brickwork.
  • A 4.5 inch wall is usually built in 1:4 mortar; 9 inch walls commonly use 1:6.
  • 1 m³ = 35.31 cubic feet (cft).

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bricks are needed for a 10 x 10 ft wall?

For a 10 ft × 10 ft wall, 9 inches thick, using standard 230 × 110 × 75 mm bricks with 10 mm mortar joints, you need about 870 bricks, or about 910 with 5% wastage. A 4.5 inch wall needs about half as many.

How many bricks are there in 1 cubic metre?

About 410 standard bricks (230 × 110 × 75 mm) or about 500 modular bricks (190 × 90 × 90 mm) per cubic metre of brickwork, including 10 mm mortar joints.

How much cement is needed for brickwork?

For a 10 × 10 ft, 9 inch wall in 1:6 cement mortar, about 2.6 bags of cement (50 kg) and about 19 cft of sand. The calculator converts wet mortar volume to dry volume using a factor of 1.33.
